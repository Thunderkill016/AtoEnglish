# Natural video candidates — A0 batch 2

Status: discovery evidence only. No item in this document is accepted for learner-facing authoring.

## Goal

Find genuinely occurring English interactions for the three weakest first-slice capabilities:

- ask another person's name;
- ask or say where someone is from;
- request repetition when speech is not heard or understood.

The batch deliberately excludes language-teaching demonstrations, scripted sketches, role-play videos, advertisements, movie dialogue, reenactments, and polished interview monologues that only look conversational.

## Naturalness contract

A candidate can enter the manual-review shortlist only when:

1. the interaction occurs during a real event, live question-and-answer session, unscripted interview, service encounter, or spontaneous social exchange;
2. the target utterance performs a real communicative function in that moment;
3. the participants are not reading a supplied English-learning script;
4. the spoken audio is English;
5. recorded evidence supports an authenticity score of at least four out of five;
6. the context is appropriate for the first A0 pilot;
7. exact playback, rights, transcript, and speaker review are still required before authoring.

Natural speech is not synonymous with useful beginner content. A real crisis call, sensitive border encounter, or highly technical exchange can be authentic and still be rejected.

## Explicit rejections

### Mandeali-language interview

The exchange appears unscripted and its file-level license claim is reusable with conditions, but the spoken audio is not English. English subtitles cannot substitute for English listening evidence.

### Border-crossing interview

The footage appears real but has non-English speech and a sensitive migration/enforcement context. It is unsuitable for the first A0 curriculum regardless of authenticity.

### Emergency crisis call

The call is genuine English interaction, but its weapon and acute-crisis context is distracting and potentially harmful for beginner learning. It is rejected rather than sanitized into a lesson.

## Rights boundary

The shortlisted Commons pages record public-domain claims associated with White House source material. This batch records those claims but does not treat them as completed item-level rights review.

A human reviewer must still check:

- the exact file page and original source;
- third-party audio, music, images, marks, or participant rights;
- attribution and source-notice requirements;
- whether the proposed transcript and derivative lesson uses are permitted;
- the exact media window and whether the file remains available.

2. 2013 and 2014 science-fair exchanges.

4. Reject or adjust any window whose audio is unclear, transcript is inaccurate, context is incomplete, or naturalness evidence does not survive playback review.

## Acceptance rule

A record may move from `shortlist_for_manual_review` to `accepted_for_authoring` only after four independent human checks pass:

```text
authenticity
+ item-level rights
+ audio and speaker identity
+ transcript and exact timestamps
```

Passing repository tests proves that the records obey this workflow. It does not prove the media is natural, legally reusable, pedagogically effective, or suitable for a learner until those reviews occur.
