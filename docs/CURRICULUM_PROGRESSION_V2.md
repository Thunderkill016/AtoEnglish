# AtoEnglish Curriculum Progression V2

Status: research-backed design contract for the rebuild branch  
Audience: curriculum authors, engineers, reviewers, and pilot evaluators  
Target learner: Vietnamese adults learning practical English for life, work, study, and travel

## 1. Product promise

A learner must not merely finish pages or collect XP. After each lesson, the learner must be able to perform one useful communicative action better than before. Across a level, the learner must show visible growth in:

1. what they understand;
2. what they can say or write;
3. how long they can sustain communication;
4. how independently they can perform;
5. how well they repair a breakdown;
6. whether they can remember and transfer the skill later.

The course therefore measures progress through task evidence, not content volume.

## 2. Research base

### CEFR action-oriented design

The Council of Europe CEFR describes proficiency through observable "can-do" actions across reception, production, interaction, and mediation. It recommends aligning planning, teaching, and assessment to selected descriptors and adapting them to learner needs and contexts.

Primary references:

- https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/cefr-descriptors
- https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/descriptive-scheme
- https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/cefr-in-the-classroom
- https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/classroom-assessment
- https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/reference-level-descriptions

AtoEnglish uses Pre-A1, A1, A2, B1, and B2 internally. "A0" remains only as a legacy route label during migration.

### English-specific language content

CEFR is intentionally language-neutral. English Profile research supplies corpus-informed evidence about words, meanings, phrases, and learner language at A1–B2. This supports level-appropriate English selection but does not mean every frequent word must be taught.

Primary references:

- Capel (2010), A1–B2 vocabulary: insights and issues arising from the English Profile Wordlists project
- English Vocabulary Profile / Cambridge Learner Corpus research

Rule: select language because it enables the mission. Use frequency and CEFR evidence to check suitability, not to create long isolated lists.

### Retrieval, spacing, and task repetition

Repeated retrieval supports vocabulary learning, and oral task repetition can improve fluency. Immediate repetition alone does not prove durable learning; delayed recall and transfer tasks are required. Excessive massed repetition can also produce mechanical verbatim repetition.

Primary references:

- Nakata (2016), within-session repeated retrieval and L2 vocabulary learning
- Lambert, Kormos, and Minn (2017), task repetition and L2 speech processing
- Suzuki and Hanzawa (2022), massed task repetition as a double-edged sword
- Suzuki et al. (2024), distributed practice and L2 fluency development

Ato rule:

- attempt 1: baseline or supported performance;
- feedback: one to three priority repairs;
- attempt 2: improved performance;
- later review: delayed recall;
- later mission: transfer to a changed scenario.

### Pronunciation for comprehensibility

Accent, intelligibility, and comprehensibility are related but not identical. A strong Vietnamese accent does not imply failure. The course prioritizes information clarity, word stress, phrase grouping, key final consonants, and interaction repair.

Primary references:

- Chau and Huensch (2025), meta-analysis of fluency, intelligibility, comprehensibility, and accentedness
- Derwing and Munro research on accent, intelligibility, and comprehensibility
- Vietnamese learner research on English final consonants and clusters

Ato rule: never score "native-likeness." Score whether the listener can understand the intended message without excessive effort.

## 3. The progression model

Every mission can move through five mastery bands:

| Band | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Introduced | The learner has encountered the mission and targets. |
| Supported | The learner completes the task with a model, frame, hints, or correction. |
| Independent | The learner completes the task without answer-level support. |
| Retained | The learner repeats the ability successfully after a delay. |
| Transfer | The learner applies the ability in a changed scenario or with new content. |

XP, streak, and quiz scores may motivate the learner, but they cannot replace this mastery model.

## 4. What "complete knowledge" means

A complete lesson is not a lesson containing every possible word or grammar rule. It is a lesson containing everything required to complete its mission and enough recycling to build durable ability.

Every production-ready core lesson must contain:

1. **Mission and role**  
   A real situation, a learner role, another participant or audience, and a concrete result.

2. **Can-do outcome**  
   One primary action that can be observed and assessed.

3. **Model input**  
   A short dialogue, message, audio, form, chart, or text that demonstrates the task.

4. **Language targets**  
   High-value chunks, grammar-as-meaning, pronunciation, pragmatics, repair, and discourse moves.

5. **Noticing**  
   The learner identifies how meaning is expressed in the model.

6. **Controlled retrieval**  
   Selection, ordering, listening discrimination, and recall move from recognition toward production.

7. **Rehearsal and personalisation**  
   The learner builds a response using their own details or a realistic role.

8. **Performance**  
   The learner completes the communicative task, not merely repeats a model sentence.

9. **Feedback and second attempt**  
   Feedback prioritizes task achievement and comprehensibility before minor accuracy.

10. **Exit and spaced review**  
    The lesson schedules core targets and a later transfer task.

The number of examples, words, dialogues, and quiz items varies by level and mission. Fixed quantity rules must not force Pre-A1 and B2 into the same lesson shape.

## 5. Level progression

### Pre-A1: survival and supported formulaic communication

**Learner state:** relies on isolated words, formulaic chunks, visuals, gestures, repetition, and a cooperative partner.

**Exit capability:** completes very short exchanges about identity and immediate needs when speech is slow and support is available.

**Language development:**

- isolated word → fixed chunk → one short clause;
- 3–5 new core chunks per lesson;
- names, numbers, prices, time, places, family, help, pain;
- fixed questions and responses before generative grammar;
- no abstract grammar explanation.

**Interaction development:**

- 2–4 turns;
- yes/no confirmation;
- spell, repeat, slower, correct/incorrect;
- use gesture and pointing appropriately.

**Pronunciation development:**

- alphabet;
- key number contrasts;
- information-bearing initial/final consonants;
- slow phrase grouping.

**Assessment:**

- 10–20 second performance;
- checklist and self-assessment;
- delayed checks after 1 and 7 days;
- success means key information is understood.

### A1: simple sentences and short familiar exchanges

**Learner state:** can generate basic one-clause sentences and use familiar expressions for concrete needs.

**Exit capability:** handles familiar transactions and short social/work exchanges with a cooperative partner.

**Language development:**

- single clause → 3–6 connected simple sentences;
- 4–7 new core targets;
- be, have, present simple, can, there is/are, basic quantity, questions;
- and, but, because, then;
- separate productive targets from optional receptive vocabulary.

**Interaction development:**

- 4–8 turns;
- ask one follow-up;
- confirm and correct details;
- simple invitation, acceptance, refusal, request, and apology.

**Pronunciation development:**

- final /s, z, t, d, k/ where meaning changes;
- plural and tense endings;
- two-syllable word stress;
- short thought groups.

**Assessment:**

- 20–40 second performance;
- familiar scenario with one changed detail;
- independent task achievement, comprehensibility, language control, and repair;
- delayed checks after 2, 7, and 21 days.

### A2: connected routine communication and simple problem solving

**Learner state:** exchanges direct information on familiar routine matters and produces short connected descriptions.

**Exit capability:** narrates experiences, negotiates plans, compares options, and solves predictable service/work problems.

**Language development:**

- sentence chain → short organised paragraph;
- 5–8 new core targets;
- past events, plans, comparisons, experience, advice, obligation;
- first, then, after that, because, so, if;
- common collocations and simple paraphrase.

**Interaction development:**

- 6–12 turns;
- propose, compare, agree, refuse, change a plan;
- explain a simple problem and request a solution;
- check and clarify details.

**Pronunciation development:**

- grammatical -ed and -s;
- common multi-syllable word stress;
- content-word prominence;
- phrasing for time order and reason.

**Assessment:**

- 40–75 second performance;
- one predictable complication;
- delayed checks after 3, 10, and 30 days;
- transfer requires changed details, not memorised replay.

### B1: connected discourse and independent familiar problem solving

**Learner state:** can get by across familiar and some less routine situations, with hesitation and circumlocution.

**Exit capability:** sustains connected speech, explains causes and consequences, defends an opinion, and proposes solutions.

**Language development:**

- short paragraph → 1–2 minute connected discourse;
- 6–10 core targets;
- narrative relations, ongoing progress, condition/consequence, process focus, precise description;
- however, although, therefore, for example, as a result;
- collocation, register awareness, and task-relevant phrasal verbs.

**Interaction development:**

- initiate, maintain, and close;
- analyse a problem;
- ask for evidence;
- negotiate actions and ownership;
- respond to a different view.

**Pronunciation development:**

- stable rhythm and pause placement;
- prominence for new information and contrast;
- clause boundaries;
- final clusters that affect meaning.

**Assessment:**

- 60–120 second performance;
- multi-step work or public scenario;
- evidence includes audio and rubric;
- delayed checks after 7, 21, and 45 days;
- checkpoint requires reception, interaction, production, and mediation.

### B2: argument, stance, synthesis, and flexible interaction

**Learner state:** can interact with reasonable fluency and develop clear detailed discourse on concrete and abstract topics.

**Exit capability:** negotiates conditions, handles hypotheticals, builds and qualifies arguments, and synthesises information for an audience.

**Language development:**

- connected discourse → structured multi-part argument or synthesis;
- 6–12 core targets with 60–80% recycled language;
- stance, hedging, concession, counterargument, evidence, limitation, conclusion;
- grammar is selected for information management and nuance;
- lexical precision, collocation, register, and paraphrase.

**Interaction development:**

- develop and challenge ideas;
- negotiate exceptions and fallbacks;
- manage politeness and firmness;
- reformulate when misunderstood;
- adapt language for audience and purpose.

**Pronunciation development:**

- thought groups in complex sentences;
- prominence for stance and contrast;
- intonation for certainty, politeness, and turn management;
- purposeful pausing.

**Assessment:**

- 90–180 second performance;
- sources or competing constraints;
- separate assessments for reception, interaction, production, and mediation;
- delayed checks after 7, 28, and 60 days;
- no automatic IELTS/TOEIC equivalence without a validation study.

## 6. Curriculum architecture

Each level follows four internal stages:

1. **Foundation** — establish the level's new sentence/discourse behavior.
2. **Expansion** — apply it to additional functions and domains.
3. **Integration** — combine skills, recycle language, and add complications.
4. **Checkpoint** — demonstrate the level in a multi-stage mission.

The 50-route scope and sequence is defined in:

- `src/lib/lessons/v2/curriculum-map.ts`
- `src/lib/lessons/v2/scope-sequence.ts`

Every mission specifies:

- communicative functions;
- core patterns;
- lexical fields;
- pronunciation focus;
- discourse and repair focus;
- previous missions recycled;
- observable performance evidence.

## 7. Progress displayed to the learner

Do not show only "12/50 lessons complete." Show a profile such as:

- 7 missions supported;
- 5 missions independent;
- 3 missions retained after delay;
- 1 mission transferred to a new situation;
- task achievement 2.4/3;
- comprehensibility 2.2/3;
- fluency 1.8/3;
- interaction repair 2.0/3.

For each can-do, show:

- **I have seen this**
- **I can do this with help**
- **I can do this independently**
- **I still can do this later**
- **I can use this in a new situation**

A level is not complete merely because all lesson routes were opened. Completion requires checkpoint performance and enough independent/retained evidence across the level's core dimensions.

## 8. Authoring constraints

### Always

- write the mission and performance task before writing vocabulary;
- select grammar because it expresses required meaning;
- recycle earlier targets explicitly;
- include a communication repair strategy;
- provide a second attempt after focused feedback;
- schedule delayed and transfer evidence;
- use Vietnamese support strategically, especially at Pre-A1–A2;
- keep examples realistic for Vietnamese adult life and work.

### Never

- use one ten-section template for every level;
- teach long vocabulary or phrasal-verb lists without a task;
- claim CEFR, IELTS, or TOEIC equivalence from lesson completion;
- score accent similarity as pronunciation quality;
- mark immediate model repetition as mastery;
- hide progress behind XP alone;
- overload checkpoint units with all skills in one 60–90 minute lesson.

## 9. Implementation order

1. Keep legacy lessons live.
2. Build V2 renderer from the discriminated step schema.
3. Build mission evidence storage and mastery calculation.
4. Author one gold lesson per level.
5. Validate content, UX, timing, and scoring.
6. Rebuild one complete level at a time.
7. Run checkpoint and delayed-retention pilot.
8. Only then migrate default routes from legacy to V2.

The first production target should be the complete Pre-A1 level, not a single isolated lesson. This proves that progression, recycling, checkpoint assessment, and delayed review work as a system.
