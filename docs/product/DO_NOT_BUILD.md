# AtoEnglish do-not-build list

**Updated:** 2026-08-02  
**Applies during:** environment-first product validation

This file prevents technically attractive work from displacing the shortest path to proving natural communication learning with an invisible curriculum.

## Source discovery and ingestion

Do not build yet:

- unrestricted paste-any-YouTube ingestion;
- unauthorized caption, transcript, audio, or video scraping;
- automatic media downloading or republishing without documented rights;
- a crawler, indexing service, media lake, or large creator catalog;
- a corpus built by searching one predetermined phrase at a time;
- automatic classification of a source as natural from platform, popularity, or production quality;
- autonomous source acceptance;
- support for every video platform;
- broad creator monetization/licensing infrastructure.

The first corpus sample must begin from complete natural recordings, then annotate communication events. Rights, naturalness, transcript, suitability, and teaching use require separate review.

## Learner-facing product

Do not build:

- grammar chapters as the primary navigation;
- a CEFR textbook rendered as a website;
- a random feed of English videos;
- standalone phrase quizzes detached from an environment;
- scripted teaching dialogues presented as natural interaction;
- a transcript-first annotated video viewer;
- an unrestricted chatbot detached from source and capability evidence;
- a social short-video feed disguised as learning;
- one-off Real Talk lessons that do not connect to an environment or transfer path.

Grammar, vocabulary, and pronunciation explanations may appear only when they help complete a practical communication goal.

## Curriculum breadth

Do not build yet:

- the complete A0–B1 curriculum;
- hundreds or thousands of clips before one environment is validated;
- a fixed clip quota that encourages low-quality or misleading sources;
- broad travel, workplace, social, interview, and exam tracks at the same time;
- a large visual curriculum CMS before event and environment contracts stabilize;
- a curriculum organized by video order;
- a curriculum organized primarily by grammar sequence;
- a native mobile app;
- clubs, multiplayer learning, creator communities, or social feeds.

Build one coherent `Meet someone new` environment first, then test whether the same contract generalizes.

## AI and language intelligence

Do not build yet:

- autonomous AI curriculum generation or publication;
- AI decisions that bypass rights, naturalness, transcript, translation, speaker, suitability, or pedagogical review;
- opaque automatic CEFR certification;
- proprietary speech recognition;
- phoneme-level pronunciation scoring infrastructure;
- accent imitation or native-likeness scoring;
- autonomous communication-event truth claims without source evidence;
- analytics containing raw learner audio, transcripts, names, employers, or personal free text.

AI may draft segmentation, event labels, capability mapping, translations, activities, and review suggestions. Humans remain responsible for publication.

## Architecture and infrastructure

Do not build yet:

- microservices for Natural Corpus, Communication Intelligence, Invisible Curriculum, or Environment Runtime;
- a second database or event platform;
- infrastructure for hypothetical catalog scale;
- a generic workflow engine;
- broad `UnitTemplate` or application rewrites unrelated to the first environment;
- generalized abstractions without at least two real environment examples;
- broad dependency upgrades mixed with product work;
- automated merge or production promotion by an agent;
- a new design system unrelated to learner blockers.

Keep the modular monolith.

## Product systems outside the current proof

Do not build yet:

- more leagues, badges, achievements, streak mechanics, or XP breadth;
- payment infrastructure redesign;
- complex subscriptions or entitlement systems;
- social/community systems;
- broad onboarding redesign;
- unrelated authentication changes;
- analytics beyond first-encounter comprehension, support use, retrieval, response, repair, transfer, delayed use, completion, and source/runtime failures.

## Premature evidence

Do not treat these as proof of product value:

- number of imported videos;
- number of generated lessons;
- number of extracted clips;
- grammar coverage percentage;
- page views;
- XP or streak length;
- multiple-choice accuracy without production or transfer;
- repository test count;
- CI/build success;
- video novelty or entertainment value;
- successful playback of one source;
- presence of a target phrase in a natural-looking clip.

Important evidence is:

- source context and naturalness integrity;
- rights and transcript integrity;
- useful communication-event coverage;
- first-encounter comprehension;
- support fading;
- retrieval without displayed answers;
- next-turn production;
- ability to repair and continue;
- different-speaker performance;
- changed-context transfer;
- delayed use;
- learner completion without facilitator language help.

## Scope mixing

Do not combine the first environment proof with unrelated changes to:

- authentication or account architecture;
- broad database or RLS redesign;
- XP, streaks, leagues, or achievements;
- payment;
- deployment automation;
- broad observability infrastructure;
- unrelated legacy lessons;
- proprietary speech systems.

Source, event, curriculum, and runtime pieces may interact in one bounded vertical slice only when coupling is required and documented.

## Exception rule

A deferred item may be reconsidered only when all are documented:

1. exact current blocker;
2. evidence the blocker occurred in a real source, environment, learner flow, or development task;
3. why a manual or simpler existing solution is insufficient;
4. smallest reversible implementation;
5. acceptance criteria and rollback plan;
6. systems explicitly left out of scope;
7. rights, naturalness, privacy, transcript, and human-review implications.

Security, privacy, rights, or data-integrity defects may interrupt the queue but still require bounded scope and verification.

## Default question

Ask:

> Does this help a learner understand, respond, continue, repair, and transfer inside a natural communication environment with less support over time?

When current evidence does not support that connection, record the idea and continue the active proof.
